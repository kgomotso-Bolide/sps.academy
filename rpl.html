Recognition of Prior Learning

Your existing skills and experience may cover a substantial portion of the credits required for a fully accredited occupational certificate

Years on the tools teach you things no classroom does. Recognition of Prior Learning — RPL — is the formal process that turns that into credits on a real qualification. Same credits, same standard, without sitting through lessons on work you can already do.

Who this is for Anyone at SPS who has been doing the work for years without the certificate to match. If you learned on site, learned from someone who never wrote it down, or trained somewhere that never registered the qualification — this is the route built for exactly that.
What it is

Assessed on what you can do, not where you learned it

The National Qualifications Framework doesn't actually care where knowledge came from. It cares whether you can demonstrate it against the standard. RPL is the process for proving that.

“The principles and processes through which the prior knowledge and skills of a person are made visible, mediated and assessed for the purpose of alternative access and admission, recognition and certification, or further learning and development.” SAQA, National Policy for the Implementation of RPL

In plain terms: you show what you know, someone qualified assesses it against the same standard everyone else is measured against, and what you can prove counts. What you can't prove yet becomes the short list of what you still need to learn — which is useful on its own.

Three things it can do

RPL isn't one thing

The QCTO recognises three distinct purposes. Which one applies depends on where you're starting from — and you may qualify for more than one.

1. Access

Gets you onto a qualification whose formal entry requirements you don't meet. Left school before matric but have twelve years of installations behind you? RPL for access is the door that opens — you're admitted on demonstrated competence instead of a school certificate.

2. Credit

Awards you actual credits towards a qualification for what you already know and can prove. You study only what's genuinely new to you. On a qualification worth 282 credits, that can be the difference between a year of study and a few months.

3. Straight to the final assessment

For occupational qualifications there's a final external exam — the EISA. RPL can admit you directly to it. If you can already do the job to standard, this is the shortest honest route to holding the certificate.

Is this you?

Four people who should be asking about RPL

If any of these describes you, it's worth a conversation with HR. It costs you nothing to ask.

You've done the job for years and never got the paper

You can fault-find, install and hand over work that passes without comment. Nobody has ever put that on a certificate, and it doesn't travel with you.

You left school early and were told that was that

Missing matric closes formal doors. RPL for access is the mechanism that was written to reopen them, and it is used for exactly this.

You trained somewhere that never registered it

In-house training, a supplier course, a programme that folded. Real learning that isn't on the NQF — which is what RPL assesses.

You did this work elsewhere and started here at the bottom

Experience from another employer, or another country, that nobody here has ever formally recognised.

A worked example. The Project Management pathway shows RPL applied to a real qualification, module by module — which of the 240 credits a free international course can put in play, which have to be studied here, and which come from work you have already done. Not sure whether you have a case? The Skills Gap check takes about two minutes and will tell you. It compares what you rate yourself at against what your role asks for, and flags it when your experience is running well ahead of your qualification — which is precisely the situation RPL exists for.

Evidence

What you'd need to show

This is called a portfolio of evidence. You don't build it alone, and you almost certainly have more of it than you think — most of it is already in your work history.

Your own work

Job cards, work orders, fault reports and hand-over sheets with your name on them. Photographs of installations you did.

People who watched you do it

A letter or testimonial from your supervisor, performance reviews, and a record of what you've actually been trusted with.

Training that never counted

Certificates from in-house or supplier courses, attendance records, anything from a programme that wasn't accredited at the time.

Being watched, and being asked

An assessor observing you on the job, and an interview about why you do it that way — understanding, not just the routine.

The process

How it runs, start to finish

You are supported through each of these. Nobody hands you a form and wishes you luck.

  1. Talk to HRA first conversation about what you can already do and which qualification it points towards. No commitment, no portfolio needed yet.
  2. Screening and adviceIs RPL the right route for you, and for which purpose — access, credit, or straight to the final assessment? Sometimes the answer is that studying it properly is the better deal, and you'd be told that.
  3. Build your portfolioGathering the evidence, with guidance on what actually counts. This is the part that takes real effort, and it's where most of your time goes.
  4. AssessmentAgainst the same standards as anyone who studied the course. It can include a written or practical assessment and being observed doing the work.
  5. ModerationThe assessment decision is checked independently. That external check is what makes the outcome worth something to an employer who has never met you.
  6. OutcomeCredits awarded, access granted, or a clear written list of what's still outstanding — and how to close it.
Straight talk

What RPL is not

Worth being clear about this up front, so nobody goes in expecting the wrong thing.

Not a shortcut past assessment

You are assessed against the same standard as everyone else. RPL changes where the evidence comes from, not how high the bar sits. A certificate that could be had for less wouldn't be worth carrying.

Not always faster

Building a portfolio is real work, and if your evidence is thin it can take longer than simply doing the course. Worth going in with your eyes open.

It can come back "not yet"

Sometimes the assessment finds real gaps. That isn't a failure — it's a costed, specific list of what to learn next, which is more than most people ever get.
